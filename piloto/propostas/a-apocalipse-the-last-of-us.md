# What Would YOUR Life Be Like in The Last of Us Apocalypse?

Proposta de roteiro (~15–16 min no ritmo do vídeo 2), **aguardando aprovação**. Cada linha numerada vira uma cena (uma frase, uma imagem).
Espinha: a sua vida, de pessoa comum, numa cidade americana, do Dia 1 até 20 anos depois. Mistura o cânone (jogo de 2013 e série da HBO de 2023) com a ciência e os dados reais de colapso (fungo Ophiocordyceps, apagão de 2003, medicina antes dos antibióticos).
Os marcadores [F1], [F2]... apontam para a tabela de fontes no fim. **Checagem feita em 2026-10-11**, ver [../../pesquisa/checagem-3-roteiros-propostos.md](../../pesquisa/checagem-3-roteiros-propostos.md). [C] = detalhe do cânone do jogo/série, confirmado só em fontes secundárias (ver o aviso lá).

Cuidados de imagem: nenhum personagem da franquia (Joel, Ellie etc.), nenhum logo, nenhuma cena copiada. Só pessoas genéricas, cidades, infectados genéricos com fungo.

## 0. Abertura (gancho: um dia normal)

1. It's an ordinary morning at the end of September.
2. You wake up, make coffee, and check the news on your way to work.
3. Something about a strange outbreak overseas. Hospitals somewhere are busy.
4. You scroll past it. Everyone does.
5. In the world of The Last of Us, that morning is the last normal one in human history. [C]
6. The game puts it in September 2013. The TV series moves it back to September 2003. [C]
7. Either way, by the end of the week, the world you know is gone.
8. Most videos about this story follow the heroes.
9. This one follows you.
10. Not a smuggler. Not a soldier. Just an ordinary person with a job, a phone, and a fridge that's half empty.
11. So let's live through it. Day one, week one, month one, year one.
12. And then, twenty years later.
13. Along the way, we'll look at what real science and real disasters tell us about how long you'd actually last.

## 1. O fungo é real (mais ou menos)

14. First, the bad news. The monster in this story is based on something real.
15. In tropical forests, there is a fungus called Ophiocordyceps unilateralis. [F1]
16. It infects carpenter ants. [F1]
17. Once it's inside, the ant's behavior changes. [F1]
18. It leaves its nest in the treetops, goes down to the low plants of the forest, and bites down hard on the vein of a leaf, on the underside. [F1]
19. Then it stays there, locked in that bite, until it dies. [F1]
20. After the ant dies, a stalk grows out of its head and releases spores onto the ground below. [F1]
21. Right where other ants walk. [F1]
22. Researchers really do call them "zombie ants." It's right in the title of the scientific paper. [F1]
23. Now the good news.
24. Most fungi can't survive inside us, because we're simply too hot. [F2]
25. Our body sits at around thirty-seven degrees Celsius, and a study of more than four thousand fungal strains found that most of them can't grow at temperatures like that. [F2]
26. Some researchers argue that our body heat works as a shield, protecting mammals from most fungi. [F2]
27. The series opens with exactly this idea: a scientist on a TV show, warning that if the planet warms, fungi might adapt to higher temperatures. [C]
28. And then, one day, our temperature wouldn't protect us anymore.
29. That part is fiction.
30. But it's the kind of fiction that scientists actually discuss. [F2]

## 2. Dia 1: a manhã em que tudo para

31. Back to you. Day one.
32. By lunchtime, the news isn't about overseas anymore.
33. In the series, the infection spreads through contaminated flour, and soon it is in many cities at once. [C]
34. In the game, it spreads through bites and through spores in the air. [C]
35. The infected don't shuffle like old movie zombies. The first ones run. [C]
36. And a bite is enough. Within hours or days, the person you knew is gone. [C]
37. Your first problem is not the infected.
38. It's the phone.
39. When something big happens, everyone calls everyone at the same moment.
40. During the great blackout of August 2003, about fifty million people in the United States and Canada lost power in a few minutes. [F3]
41. Cell networks were overwhelmed, and many calls simply didn't go through. [F3]
42. So you can't reach your family.
43. You get in the car. So does everyone else.
44. In the series, the first night is chaos on the highways: crashes, fires, people running between stopped cars. [C]
45. That part is not hard to believe.
46. Every hurricane evacuation shows the same thing: highways become parking lots.
47. So most people on day one don't go far.
48. They go home, lock the door, and wait for someone to tell them what to do.

## 3. Semana 1: tudo que precisa de gente

49. Here's something most people never think about.
50. Almost everything in your house needs someone, somewhere, to keep working.
51. The power plant needs workers. The water plant needs workers. The trucks need drivers.
52. In the 2003 blackout, gas stations couldn't pump fuel, because the pumps run on electricity. [F3]
53. In Cleveland, the pumping stations stopped, and about a million and a half people lost their water. [F3]
54. In Cleveland and Detroit, people were told to boil their water. [F3]
55. And that blackout lasted a day or two for most people, and up to four days in some places. [F3]
56. Now imagine it never comes back.
57. Food is the next problem.
58. Supermarkets don't keep much stock. They live on constant deliveries. [F4]
59. When the trucks stop, the shelves empty.
60. And everyone remembers early 2020, when toilet paper disappeared in a weekend.
61. So by the end of week one, you're counting cans.
62. You've filled the bathtub with water, because someone online said to.
63. That actually is official advice: store water before the taps fail. [F5]
64. U.S. emergency agencies recommend at least one gallon of water per person, per day. [F5]
65. Outside, the city is changing. Fewer cars. More sirens. Then, fewer sirens.
66. And somewhere, the government is making a decision about you.

## 4. Mês 1: a zona de quarentena

67. In this world, the government doesn't try to save every city.
68. It picks a few, and puts walls around them.
69. These are the quarantine zones. QZs, for short. [C]
70. Boston. Pittsburgh. Seattle. Kansas City. A handful of others. [C]
71. Everything outside the walls is left to the infected.
72. In the series, the agency that runs them is called FEDRA: the Federal Disaster Response Agency. [C]
73. If you live near one, you're lucky. Or you think you are.
74. Getting in means lining up at a gate with hundreds of strangers.
75. Soldiers check everyone for infection. Anyone who fails the test doesn't get in. [C]
76. And in this story, "doesn't get in" is a polite way of saying something much worse.
77. Inside, your new life begins.
78. You get a bed in an old building, often shared with strangers.
79. There is a curfew. Break it, and you're in serious trouble. [C]
80. There are regular checks. Everyone is a suspect, all the time.
81. Money is worthless now.
82. What matters is ration cards. [C]
83. You work, and you get cards. You trade the cards for food.
84. And the jobs are the ones nobody wants.
85. In the series, people in the zone earn ration cards by working jobs for the zone. [C]
86. Hauling. Cleaning. Repairs. Whatever the soldiers need done.
87. It's hard to complain, though.
88. Every person inside the wall knows exactly what's outside it.

## 5. Ano 1: o que acontece com os infectados

89. By the end of the first year, the infected outside are changing too.
90. In this world, the fungus doesn't just take over the brain. It keeps growing. [C]
91. The newly infected are called runners. Fast, aggressive, still almost human. [C]
92. After a while, they become stalkers: they hide, and they wait. [C]
93. Then comes the stage everyone fears.
94. After a year or more, the fungus splits the face open, and covers the eyes. [C]
95. These are the clickers. They can't see anymore. [C]
96. So they hunt the way bats and dolphins do: with sound. [C]
97. They make a clicking noise, and listen to the echo. [C]
98. And that idea is real too.
99. Some blind people actually learn to navigate this way, by clicking their tongue and listening to the echoes around them. [F6]
100. Which means that in this world, the most important survival skill isn't shooting.
101. It's being quiet.
102. After many years, some infected become bloaters: huge, armored by layers of fungus, and very hard to kill. [C]
103. And when an infected finally dies, the fungus doesn't.
104. In the game, it grows on the walls and releases clouds of spores. You'll want a gas mask. [C]
105. In the series, it mostly spreads through long tendrils underground, connecting the infected like a network. [C]
106. Remember the ant, biting the leaf?
107. The real fungus doesn't care about the ant. It cares about the next ant. [F1]
108. That's exactly what the fictional one does too.

## 6. Ano 1: o que você come, o que você faz o dia todo

109. So what does a normal day look like for you, one year in?
110. You wake up in the zone at dawn. There's no alarm clock, because there's barely any electricity. [C]
111. Breakfast is whatever the cards bought. Often the same thing every day.
112. Then you report for work.
113. Maybe you're on a cleaning crew. Maybe you repair walls. Maybe you're lucky, and you work in a kitchen.
114. In the afternoon, there are lines. Lines for food, lines for water, lines for medicine.
115. And there's a black market.
116. In zones like these, people trade everything: pills, batteries, alcohol, cigarettes, information.
117. Every disaster in real history has created one.
118. Under wartime rationing in Britain, for example, there was an illegal trade in rationed goods, and people even had a name for the traders: spivs. [F7]
119. In the evening, curfew.
120. You go back to your room, eat, talk to whoever shares it with you, and go to sleep early.
121. There's no TV, no internet, no new music.
122. Just the sound of the wall, and the soldiers walking on it.
123. And here's the strange thing.
124. After a few months, it stops feeling strange.
125. People get used to almost anything. Historians of every war and siege say the same thing.
126. Life becomes routine. Even this.

## 7. Fora do muro

127. But not everyone stays inside.
128. Some people hate the zones: the rules, the executions, the soldiers.
129. In this story, there's a rebel group called the Fireflies, fighting the military government. [C]
130. Some people simply leave and try their luck outside.
131. Out there, the infected are only one of the dangers.
132. There are raiders and hunters: groups who survive by robbing, or killing, other survivors. [C]
133. And there are small communities that actually work.
134. In the series, there's a town in Jackson, Wyoming, with walls, farms, horses, and electricity from a nearby dam. [C]
135. That's maybe the most realistic part of the whole story.
136. When big systems collapse, people tend to rebuild in small groups, with whatever they have nearby.
137. Water from a river. Power from a dam. Food from a field.
138. Life starts to look less like 2013, and more like 1850.

## 8. O inimigo silencioso: o remédio que acaba

139. And in 1850, the biggest killer wasn't monsters.
140. It was infection. The ordinary kind.
141. In this world, nobody is making new antibiotics.
142. Factories are gone, and the medicine that's left is expiring on the shelves.
143. Before antibiotics, a small cut on your hand could kill you.
144. Penicillin wasn't discovered until 1928, and it wasn't widely available until the 1940s. [F8]
145. Before that, things like pneumonia and infected wounds killed huge numbers of people. [F9]
146. In 1900, in the United States, about one in ten babies died before their first birthday. [F9]
147. Childbirth was one of the most dangerous things a woman could do.
148. And in the apocalypse, all of that comes back.
149. No dentist. A bad tooth can become a deadly infection.
150. No glasses, if yours break.
151. No insulin for diabetics. No dialysis. No surgery that needs electricity.
152. This is the part the stories usually skip.
153. Most people wouldn't die from a bite.
154. They'd die from a scratch on a rusty fence, or a fever in the winter.

## 9. A natureza volta

155. Meanwhile, the cities are going green.
156. And here, we actually know a lot about what would happen.
157. Without pumps, the New York subway would start to flood, because water constantly leaks into its tunnels and has to be pumped out every day. [F10]
158. Without people, grass breaks through the asphalt. Trees grow on rooftops. Rust eats the bridges.
159. We have a real example.
160. After the Chernobyl nuclear disaster in 1986, people abandoned a huge area around the plant. [F11]
161. Decades later, a long-term census found elk, deer and wild boar as abundant as in nearby nature reserves, and wolves more than seven times more numerous. [F11]
162. Even Przewalski's horses live there now, though they were released on purpose, in 1998. [F11]
163. The researchers don't say radiation is good for wildlife. They say the effect of human presence, with hunting, farming and forestry, is a lot worse. [F11]
164. In the world of The Last of Us, you see the same thing: giraffes walking through a ruined city, deer in the streets. [C]
165. It's beautiful. And it's a reminder of how fast the world forgets us.

## 10. Vinte anos depois

166. Now, twenty years have passed.
167. In the game, it's 2033. In the series, 2023. [C]
168. If you survived, you're a different person.
169. You know how to stay quiet. You know how to stretch a can of beans for two days.
170. You know which streets are safe, and which buildings you never, ever enter.
171. And there's a new generation who never knew the old world.
172. Kids who have never seen a working airplane.
173. Who have never seen a movie, or eaten ice cream, or ridden in a car on a highway.
174. For them, the stories adults tell about the old world sound like fairy tales.
175. Cities that glowed all night. Food that arrived on trucks. A phone that could talk to anyone on Earth.
176. And slowly, knowledge disappears.
177. Who still knows how to make penicillin? How to fix a power line? How to build an engine?
178. Every year, the people who remember die, and some of that knowledge dies with them.
179. Historians have seen this before.
180. After the Western Roman Empire fell, many building skills disappeared from parts of Western Europe for centuries. [F12]
181. In Britain, for example, mass-produced pottery disappeared after the Romans left, and for centuries almost nobody built in stone. [F12]
182. A civilization doesn't need to be destroyed to go backwards.
183. It just needs to stop teaching.

## 11. Então, você sobreviveria?

184. So, would you survive?
185. Honestly? Probably not the first week.
186. Most people in a disaster this fast don't die because they're weak or stupid.
187. They die because they're in the wrong place, at the wrong time.
188. But if you made it into a zone, and you kept your head down, and you never got a cut infected...
189. Then your life would look a lot like the lives of most people in human history.
190. Hard work. Simple food. Short nights. Little news from the outside world.
191. Rules made by people you never voted for.
192. And a small group of people you trust.
193. In a way, that's what makes The Last of Us frightening.
194. It's not that the world becomes something new.
195. It's that the world goes back to something very, very old.
196. And here's the last thing the real fungus can teach us.
197. The zombie ant fungus works because the ant stops behaving like part of its colony. [F1]
198. It leaves the others, and walks away alone.
199. In the story, the fungus never wins because it's smart.
200. It wins every time people stop trusting each other.
201. Maybe that's the real survival skill.
202. Not the gun. Not the gas mask.
203. Each other.
204. If you liked this, tell me in the comments: how long do you think you'd last?
205. And which "ordinary life" should we live through next?
